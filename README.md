# Aircraft Screws, Bolts & Nuts: Aerospace Fasteners for Aircraft Maintenance and MRO

Aircraft screws, bolts, and nuts are fundamental mechanical fasteners used throughout airframe structures, engines, landing gear, flight-control assemblies, equipment installations, and cabin systems. Despite their small size, these components perform important functions by joining parts, maintaining alignment, providing clamping force, and allowing assemblies to remain secure under vibration and repeated operational loads.

Unlike general-purpose commercial hardware, aircraft fasteners are selected according to specific dimensions, materials, strength requirements, locking characteristics, finishes, and applicable specifications. Threaded fasteners may be identified through standards such as AN, NAS, and MS designations, along with manufacturer or supplier part numbers. Accurate identification is therefore important when replacing fasteners during aircraft maintenance.

For aviation parts distributors and MRO procurement teams, sourcing the correct screw, bolt, or nut requires more than matching appearance. Thread size, length, head style, material, finish, locking method, installation requirements, and the application in which the fastener will be installed should all be considered.

## Aircraft Bolts for Structural and Mechanical Applications

Aircraft bolts are commonly used where a strong, controlled mechanical joint is required. Depending on the design and application, bolts can be found in structural connections, landing gear assemblies, engine installations, control mechanisms, equipment mounts, and other aircraft systems. General-purpose, close-tolerance, internal-wrenching, and specialty bolt configurations may be encountered across aviation applications.

Bolt identification can include head markings, specification numbers, dimensions, material information, and manufacturer details. The correct bolt diameter and grip length are particularly important because the fastener must properly accommodate the materials being joined while providing the required clamping arrangement.

## Aircraft Screws for Serviceable Assemblies

Aircraft screws are used in many applications where components need to be installed and removed during inspection, servicing, or maintenance. They can be found on access panels, fairings, avionics equipment, interior assemblies, equipment covers, and other installations where repeated removal may be required.

Common aircraft screw configurations include machine screws, structural screws, self-tapping screws, and specialty head styles. Head configuration can vary according to the surrounding structure and the available installation space. Countersunk designs, for example, can provide a flush installation where surface clearance or aerodynamic considerations require it.

## Aircraft Nuts and Locking Methods

Aircraft nuts provide the threaded mating component for many bolts and screws. Common configurations include plain nuts, castellated nuts, self-locking nuts, shear nuts, and anchor nuts. The appropriate type depends on the joint design, loading conditions, installation configuration, and applicable maintenance requirements.

Because aircraft experience vibration and repeated loading, nut retention is an important consideration. Depending on the application, retention can involve self-locking designs, castellated nuts with cotter pins, safety wire arrangements, or other approved methods. The applicable maintenance documentation determines which locking method is appropriate for a particular installation.

A nut that has the correct thread diameter but the wrong locking or material specification may not be an appropriate replacement.

## Materials, Finishes, and Environmental Requirements

Aircraft screws, bolts, and nuts can be manufactured from materials such as alloy steel, corrosion-resistant steel, aluminum alloys, and titanium, depending on the intended application. Material selection can affect strength, weight, corrosion resistance, temperature capability, and compatibility with surrounding components.

Surface finishes and coatings may also form part of the fastener specification. Aircraft installations can expose hardware to moisture, temperature changes, vibration, fluids, and other environmental conditions. Corrosion-resistant materials and appropriate protective finishes can therefore be important considerations in fastener selection.

## Fastener Identification During Aircraft Maintenance

Fastener identification is an important part of aircraft maintenance because similar-looking screws, bolts, and nuts can differ in strength, dimensions, thread characteristics, material, and locking features. A part number, specification designation, engineering drawing, illustrated parts catalog, or applicable maintenance documentation can provide the information needed for accurate identification.

During inspection or replacement work, technicians may encounter worn threads, damaged heads, corrosion, deformation, or evidence of previous improper installation. These conditions can require replacement with the correct approved hardware rather than simply reusing or substituting an available commercial fastener.
